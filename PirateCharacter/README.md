# 🏴‍☠️ Pirate Character — a complete low-poly 3D character

A pirate character **modeled, textured and rigged** in Blender from a single piece of
reference art. Everything is generated by script — you can wipe the whole scene and
rebuild the character from nothing with one command.

## 🦴 The rig

A standard engine hierarchy, with `.L`/`.R` suffixes (Blender recognizes the symmetry):

```
root
└── pelvis
    ├── spine_01 → spine_02 → spine_03
    │   ├── neck → head
    │   └── clavicle.L/R → upperarm → lowerarm → hand
    │       ├── finger_index/middle/ring/pinky_01 → _02
    │       └── thumb_01 → thumb_02
    └── thigh.L/R → calf → foot → toe
```

Hands with **articulated fingers** (2 bones each + thumb), so the character can close a
fist, hold a weapon and point.

### How the skinning was solved

Automatic weights alone are not enough on a character made of separate pieces. Three
correction passes run afterwards:

1. **Orphan vertices** → nearest bone (point-to-segment distance). The heat weighting
   solver does not reach isolated islands.
2. **Bone whitelist per piece** → the coat may only use `pelvis`/`spine`/`clavicle`.
   Without that, crouching pulled the torso along with the thigh.
3. **Trim stuck to its neighbor** → buckles, buttons and lapels inherit the weight of
   the nearest vertex *outside* their own island, otherwise they float off on the first
   pose.

## 🏃 The animations

Two cycles, both generated by the same engine (`anim_gait.py`). The legs are **not posed
by hand**: you describe the foot's trajectory and the knee comes out of a two-link IK
solved by the law of cosines.

| | 🚶 Walk | 🏃 Run |
|---|---|---|
| Stride duration | 0.800 s (24 f) | 0.600 s (18 f) |
| Step | 0.66 m | 1.10 m |
| Cadence | 150 steps/min | 200 steps/min |
| **Native speed** | **1.65 m/s** | **3.67 m/s** |
| Stance | 58% of the cycle | 31% |
| Frames in flight | none | **6 of 18** |
| Body rise and fall | 4.2 cm | 6.9 cm |
| Foot slip | **0.003 mm** | **0.004 mm** |
| Boot inside the deck | 1 mm | 0 mm |

### What keeps the foot from skating

During stance, the foot moves backwards **at exactly the body's speed** — a straight
line in time, not a smoothed curve. The rest of the cycle folds around that: the body's
height is the highest the leg reaches without tearing the foot off the ground, and the
ankle's height comes from the envelope of the rotated sole, so the boot rolls over the
deck instead of going through it.

> [!note] The body does not chase the leg's limit
> The first version pinned the hip to the maximum height frame by frame and the result
> jumped **7 cm in a single frame**: at the end of stance the straightened leg let the
> body rise, and on the next frame the other foot touched the ground and demanded all of
> it back. A real body describes a smooth curve that passes *below* the limit — which is
> what the script does now.

### Running is not walking fast

Three things change in kind, not in degree:

1. **The flight phase.** With stance at 31%, six of the eighteen frames have no foot on
   the ground at all. It is what allows a long step without the body having to come
   down.
2. **The vertical curve inverts.** Walking, the body is low with the legs apart and high
   passing over the foot. Running, it is low **in the middle of stance** (the leg absorbs
   like a spring) and high **in the middle of the flight**.
3. **The foot lands almost under the body.** Landing ahead is *overstriding* — the foot
   becomes a brake. One third of the excursion in front, two thirds behind.

### Why neither one matches the game's speed

`WALK_SPEED` is 2.8 m/s and `RUN_SPEED` is 4.7 m/s; the clips walk at 1.65 and run at
3.67. That is not sloppiness, it is what this leg reaches: with 0.788 m between hip and
ankle, walking at 2.8 m/s would require a 1.12 m step, and the hip would have to drop so
far for the foot to get there that the walk would become a crouching shuffle.

The correction happens at runtime, with `timeScale = speed / native_speed` — and, in the
middle of the range, by **blending the two clips** instead of stretching one.

### 🪜 Climbing

The walk's vertical cousin, and with the same contract: **the phase advances with the
height gained, not with the clock**. While the hand holds, the rung is stationary in the
world — in the body's frame it comes down in a straight line, at exactly the speed of the
climb. Four contacts instead of one, and the ruler is right there on screen: the rung.

| | 🪜 ClimbUp |
|---|---|
| Cycle | 1.0 s (30 f) — **two ratlines**, 60.67 cm |
| Native speed | 0.607 m/s (2 rungs/s) |
| Stance | hand 58% of the cycle, foot 46% |
| Hand / foot slip | **0.000 mm** / **0.002 mm** |
| Hand wrapping the rung | 2.5 cm |
| Max extension arm / leg | 85% / 96% |

The cycle measures two rungs because the ship's ladder measures **30.33 cm** between
rungs — the `round` in `ShipParts.ts` rounds the number of gaps, not the spacing. Since
the rise per cycle is an integer multiple of that, the phase can be **married to the
absolute height** on grabbing: the hand lands on top of the rung that is drawn, and keeps
landing there for all nine meters. Going down is the same clip with the phase running
backwards, which guarantees the descent's contacts use the same grid as the climb.

> [!warning] Two skinning defects this clip dug up
> Climbing lifts the knee to 90° and the shoulder to 9° — no earlier animation came close
> to that. Two wrong weight inheritances that had been latent from the start showed up:
> six islands of the coat with up to **97% weight on the thigh** (the skirt rose with the
> leg) and the **clavicle with 35% influence on the coat skirt and 50% on the torso**
> (lifting the shoulder swung the whole garment). The corrections are in `build_rig.py`
> and apply to every clip.

> [!note] Measuring the constructed pose proves nothing
> The `build` metrics measured perfect contact while the character was gripping thin air
> five centimeters beside the rung. `anim_gait`'s `_key_frame` does not record the
> clavicle — and the arm's IK had solved the pose *over the displaced shoulder*. Hence
> `verify()`: it re-reads the **recorded action**, deforms the mesh and measures the
> hand's geometry against the rung's cylinder.

### 🎡 Helm

The third clip married to the ship's geometry, and the cleanest fit of the three. The
wheel has **eight spoke handles** and turns **exactly once** from stop to stop, so one
cycle of the clip covers **45° — one handle step** — and the phase comes straight out of
the rudder angle:

```
phase = frac(wheel_angle / (π/4))
```

No initial alignment. The ladder needs one (`ClimbClock.align`) because the grid of rungs
is a ruler in the world and the phase has to be married to it on grabbing; here the grid
**is** the angle itself, so the hand lands on top of a handle that is drawn at any rudder
position, including when you start turning from the middle. Turning to port is the same
clip with the phase running backwards.

| | 🎡 Helm |
|---|---|
| Cycle | 0.833 s (25 f) — **45° of wheel**, one handle |
| Indexed by | **wheel angle** (`wheelAngle`) |
| Right / left hand arc | 56.7°–87.3° / 92.7°–123.3° |
| Max arm extension | **87.5%** |
| Hand wrapping the handle | 2.23 cm |
| Palm slip / cycle | 0.32 mm |
| **Hand drift over the full 360° of travel** | **0.53 mm** |
| Torso clearance to the wood | 11.7 cm |
| Regimes | 48% with both hands · 40% with one · 12% with neither |

> [!warning] The station was 20 cm beyond the arm
> `HELM_STAND` put the helmsman **0.85 m** from the plane of the wheel. This rig's arm is
> **0.662 m** from shoulder to fingertip, and the shoulder sits 1.462 m above the feet —
> the distance to the top handle came out at **0.862 m**. In first person it got worse:
> the body's 11 cm setback *adds* to the gap, because at the helm the body points at the
> bow.
>
> The 0.85 m was never an arm measurement. It was chosen for framing, back when the
> player was still a camera with no body — and the defect did not show, because at the
> helm the character played `Idle` with empty hands. **The bug was born together with the
> animation that was supposed to hide it.** The station came in to 0.62 m, and the helm
> blocker's radius dropped from 0.5 to 0.32 with it, otherwise the player gets pushed out
> of the station on walking up to it.

> [!note] The fingers do not close around the handle
> The wheel's handle is **Ø 9.5 cm** against a hand of 5.8 × 3.0 cm. The pose is a cupped
> hand over the top, not a closed fist — 2.2 cm of wrap is the most the anatomy allows. A
> real ship's wheel handle is about 4 cm; thinning `createWheel` would settle it for
> good, and both variants are worth having.

The rejected variant is still reproducible: `anim_helm.build(anim_helm.INTACT)` records
`_HelmIntact`, with the original 0.85 m station and the torso bent 18° to reach. The `_`
prefix makes `export.py` discard it on its own.

## 🌊 The two water clips

In the land clips `z = 0` is the ground, under the feet. In these two it is the
**waterline** — the body is split by it, and it is the `root` that positions it. Neither
one animates the rise and fall over the wave: what lifts the avatar is the runtime, and
repeating that here would give two motions stacked on top of each other.

### 🛟 Float — treading water

The water's `Idle`, and the first clip in the folder **without a single point of
contact**.

| | 🛟 Float |
|---|---|
| Cycle | 7.0 s (210 f) — **3 leg beats · 5 arm sweeps · 2 breaths** |
| Chin clearance to the surface | **11.83 cm** on the worst frame |
| Shoulder above the waterline | 11.23 cm |
| Submerged fraction | 56.3%, spread 1.45 pp |
| Vertical micro-oscillation | 2.48 cm (ceiling of 3 cm) |
| Thigh ahead of **world** vertical | 5° – 30° – 53° |
| Knee angle | 105° – 117° – 134° |
| Ankle orbit (lateral / fore-aft / vertical) | 47.6 / 35.0 / 17.5 cm |
| Max extension leg / arm | 92.4% / 90.3% |

The three periods are **pairwise coprime**: 3, 5 and 2 only meet again after the full
7 seconds, so the eye does not time the loop. It is the same defense `anim_idle` promises
in its comment and does not deliver — there the `build` multiplies the sway back to the
breathing's period.

The leg beat is an **eggbeater**: each leg sweeps a cone around an axis hanging from the
hip, and the two sweep in **opposite directions** — hence the name, and it is from that
opposition that the torque cancellation keeping the body facing forward comes. The
abduction is mirrored between the sides; the flexion is not. Only that asymmetry produces
the counter-rotation.

> [!warning] The first version drew a chair, and the attempted fix made it worse
> The leg beat was born as an **ellipse in the sagittal plane** — pedaling slowly — and a
> sagittal ellipse always reads as pedaling *while seated*. The defect was identified, and
> the attempted fix was to recline the torso from 18° to 22°, noting that "every degree of
> recline **removes** a degree from the thigh's inclination in the world".
>
> It is the opposite. Reclining rotates the body's frame backwards, and a vector that
> pointed downwards starts pointing downwards **and forwards**: the recline **adds**.
> Measured on the recorded action, the thigh was not at 26° — it was at **71° ahead of
> world vertical**, 89° on the worst frame, meaning horizontal. The 4° bought 4° more
> chair and lowered the chin as well, because the head is *above* the recline pivot.
>
> The fix was three things at once: taking the leg out of the sagittal plane (knee
> **outwards**, like a frog, instead of forwards), describing the ankle at a **nearly
> constant radius** so the knee does not close, and putting the recline back to 15°.
> Thigh: 71° → **30°**. Knee: 98° → **117°**.

> [!note] Measuring in the body's frame proves nothing
> The same family of defect as the climbing note, one floor up: there the metric measured
> the *constructed* pose instead of the recorded one; here it measured the right angle in
> the **wrong frame**. The leg's constants are written in the body because that is what
> keeps a clip with no contact at all coherent while the body turns — but the reading of
> "seated" happens in the world, which is where the camera is. `verify()` now returns
> `thigh_pitch_deg` and `knee_angle_deg` read off the action, in world space.

The arms do **sculling**: they sweep the water in a lying figure eight with the palm
turned **against its own movement** and angled downwards. The palm rule is what sculling
*is* — the hand becomes an oar, and the downward component of the force holds the body up.
It comes from the trajectory by finite difference, so it stays right if anyone changes the
figure eight.

The depth is deliberately chosen **above physical equilibrium**. A human body treading
water has the water at the chin; this one has the water at the chest. The runtime only
knows the wave height approximately, and the cost of erring downwards is the head going
into the water — the one thing the game promises does not happen.

### 🏊 Swim — head-up crawl

The game does not allow diving, and that rule chooses the stroke: the competition crawl
breathes to the side and spends half the cycle with the face in the water, which leaves
the **head-up crawl**, the rescue stroke. Same alternating stroke, more inclined torso,
extended neck, roll cut in half.

| | 🏊 Swim |
|---|---|
| Cycle | 1.0 s (30 f) — one stroke, 120 arms/min |
| Indexed by | **distance covered** — 1.32 m per cycle |
| **Native speed** | **1.32 m/s** (game: 1.40 → `timeScale` 1.06) |
| Hand sweep through the water | 110.2 cm per arm |
| Propulsive efficiency | 0.599 |
| Hand in the water | 60% of the cycle per arm |
| Torso inclination | 19.8° (**solved for**, not chosen) |
| Face clearance to the surface | 3.51 cm · neck +0.39 cm |
| Body submerged | 51.3% – 55.8% |
| Kick | 6 beats/cycle, 38.2 cm of ankle excursion |
| Max extension arm / leg | 97.2% / 98.1% |
| Loop closure | **0.000 mm** |

The origin falls on the **sternum**, not under the feet: rotating the body without
translating would leave the origin at the heels and the avatar would show up a meter and
a half ahead of the player's position.

> [!note] The face clearance is an **input** to the problem, not a check afterwards
> `solve_attitude()` does not choose the torso's attitude: it bisects over it, posing the
> body, deforming the mesh through the depsgraph and reading the lowest point of the head,
> until the requested clearance comes out. The cervical extension is fixed at the
> anatomical 42° because it **has a stop** (~70°, and near it the neck reads as broken);
> what adjusts is the torso, which only costs drag — which is exactly the currency this
> stroke spends.

> [!warning] The pitch pivoted at the feet
> The `root`'s two rotations turn around the rig's origin, and before the translation the
> origin is **at the feet**. A 1.4° pitch per stroke around a pivot 1.13 m from the
> sternum moved the body **2.8 cm**: the face, with 4.7 cm of clearance in the neutral
> pose, touched the water on frame 14. Applied after the displacement, the pivot becomes
> the sternum itself and the pitch moves 1.2 mm.

> [!note] Arching is worth twice as much as extending the neck
> Every 6° of arch buys 6.5° of torso inclination; every 10° of neck buys 3 — arching
> rotates the whole body above the lumbar, and the head goes with it. With an 8° arch the
> contact sheet showed somebody **diving downhill**, at 28°. With 16° it is 19.8°, the
> middle of the head-up crawl's real range (15–25°).

What makes the stroke read as water and not as gymnastics is the **irregular speed along
the path** — the hand enters at 1.6 m/s, the push goes to 4.2 m/s, the recovery crosses
loose and decelerates to enter — and the **palm turning through the gesture**: edge-on at
the entry, backwards at the catch, inwards on the S sweep, upwards at the exit, loose on
the recovery. The tracks interpolate by **cyclic Catmull-Rom** and not by `smoothstep`
between keys: `smoothstep` zeroes the derivative at every key, and eleven keys a second
would be the hand stopping eleven times per stroke.

## 🎨 Textures

The materials are **procedural** (noise + edge highlighting from the geometry's
*pointiness*) and then **baked** to a 4K atlas. That is what gives the wear along the
edges without painting anything by hand.

| Map | Suffix | Space |
|---|---|---|
| Base Color | `_D` | sRGB |
| Metallic | `_M` | Non-Color |
| Roughness | `_R` | Non-Color |
| Normal | `_N` | Non-Color |
| Ambient Occlusion | `_AO` | Non-Color |

> [!warning] Base color is baked by EMIT, not by DIFFUSE
> The `DIFFUSE` pass of a material with `Metallic = 1` returns **black** — metal has no
> diffuse component. Every buckle came out black on the first attempt. The solution is to
> reroute the socket to an Emission node and bake with `type="EMIT"`.

## 🔁 Rebuilding from scratch

With Blender open and the MCP addon connected:

```python
import sys
sys.path.insert(0, r"...\PirateCharacter\scripts")

import build_all, materials, finalize, build_rig, export
import anim_walk, anim_run, anim_idle, anim_jump, anim_climb, anim_helm
import anim_carry, anim_float, anim_swim

build_all.run()              # geometry (wipes the scene first)
materials.apply_materials()  # procedural materials per object/face
finalize.run()               # join + UV + atlas bake + final material
build_rig.run()              # armature + skinning + weight corrections
anim_walk.build()            # walk cycle
anim_run.build()             # run cycle
anim_idle.build()            # standing, breathing
anim_jump.build()            # JumpAir + JumpLand
anim_climb.build()           # climb cycle
anim_helm.run()              # both helm variants (Helm + _HelmIntact)
anim_carry.build()           # the plank held across the body
anim_float.build()           # floating
anim_swim.build()            # swimming
export.run()                 # validates and exports .blend / .fbx / .glb
```

> [!note] Having Blender open is a convenience, not a requirement
> The whole pipeline runs **headless**, and that is how it has been run in the last few
> rounds — including two clips being worked on at the same time, in separate processes,
> which the addon's single instance would not allow:
>
> ```sh
> blender --background pirate_character.blend --python-expr "
> import sys, os; sys.path.insert(0, os.path.join(os.getcwd(), 'scripts'))
> import anim_float; print(anim_float.build()); print(anim_float.verify())"
> ```
>
> The only part that **requires** a GUI is `anim_preview.py`, which records the viewport
> through `render.opengl`. For video without a screen there is `playblast.py`, which
> renders through an orthographic camera — and, as a bonus, leaves the framing written down
> instead of inherited from `view_selected`, so two clips compared use the same ruler.

After touching the climb, it is worth running both checks — they measure different things
and the first has lied once already:

```python
anim_climb.verify()          # re-reads the action and measures the hand against the rung
anim_climb.clearance()       # how far the body goes into the ladder
anim_climb.bake_preview()    # assembles the navigable climb so you can hit Play
```

The helm has the same checks, plus one the ladder did not need — the sweep over the
rudder's whole travel:

```python
anim_helm.verify(anim_helm.NEAR)       # measures the palm against the handle's cylinder
anim_helm.sweep_check(anim_helm.NEAR)  # 361 rudder angles: how far does the hand drift?
anim_helm.clearance(anim_helm.NEAR)    # how far the body goes into the wheel
```

> [!warning] Pass the variant explicitly
> The `variant` parameter still defaults to `INTACT`, and `INTACT` is the **rejected**
> variant. Calling `anim_helm.verify()` with no argument measures the pose that is not in
> the game, and it passes everything — it is just measuring something else.

The whole pipeline runs in **~60 seconds**.

## 🧠 How the likeness was obtained

No "modeling by eye". The proportions came from **measuring the reference art**:
anchoring the top of the skull and the sole in px and converting to a target height of
1.80 m gives a scale of `1 px = 0.00178 m` — and from there every landmark.

That is how the traits that make the character look like *him* appeared:

- 🦵 **Short legs** — crotch at 44% of the height (average human: 47–48%). That is where
  the stocky look comes from.
- 🎽 **Hips wider than the shoulders** — because of the belt and the coat's skirts.
- 🎩 **A 20 cm tricorne**, with the brim folded in three sectors and the points passing
  above the crown.
- 🧔 **A beard with a variable upper edge** — down below the lip in front and rising to the
  sideburn at the sides, following the real line of the jaw.

### Face details

![face](preview/step10_face.png)

Every feature is **anchored mathematically on the skull's surface** (the function inverts
the superellipse's parameterization to find the skin's Y at each X and Z). Positioning "by
eye" sank the geometry inside the head and it simply vanished from the render.

## ⚠️ Known limitations

- **Clips are missing.** There are walk, run, idle, jump, climb, helm, plank, float and
  swim; the cannon and the bilge pump are missing.
- **`Idle` is the heaviest clip in the GLB** — 487 KB of the file's 3.2 MB. It is the
  direct price of hiding the loop: stretching the cycle to 9.6 s tripled the keys.
  Shortening it gives back the space and gives back the defect with it.
- **The coat's collar grazes the beard while swimming**, 0.74 cm. It is not the clip's
  fault: the published `Walk` does 0.81 and 1.01 on the same pairs. It is the same weight
  inheritance the climb dug up, and the fix is in `build_rig.py`, applying to all of them.
- **The tricorne swims along.** Nobody takes their hat off before falling into the sea,
  and in game it is probably worth hiding it in the water.
- **The helm does not read the rudder's absolute angle**, only the phase. A helmsman
  bracing against a hard-over turn would take a second clip or an additive layer — and the
  engine has no additive today.
- **12% of the helm's cycle has no hand on the wheel.** That is the price of the two hands
  not fighting over the same handle: with a wider window the measurement found 6 cm
  between the palms, against 9 cm of hand width — they went through each other. Standing
  still, there is a 12% chance the rudder freezes on a frame with one hand in the air.
- **There is no `ClimbHold`.** Standing still on the ladder, the runtime freezes the phase
  and the character stays gripping exactly where they were — no sliding, but no breathing
  either. A "gripping and breathing" would only work as an **additive** layer: as a full
  pose it would send all four grips to its own rungs and drag the hands up to 15 cm on the
  transition.
- **Arm and leg graze the stile** while climbing, up to 3.7 cm on isolated frames. With
  the body 29 cm from the ladder and the stiles 24 to each side, some limb passes through
  there on the way to the rung — four grip widths were tested and the number changes
  owner, not size.
- **No LODs.** For in-game use with many characters on screen, it is worth generating
  LOD1/LOD2.
- **No facial blend shapes.** The head's topology is that of a stylized character, with no
  concentric loops at the eye and the mouth — facial expression would take retopologizing
  the head.
- **Pieces interpenetrate** (boot × trouser, buckle × belt). It is normal practice in

  geometry.
- **No control rig** (IK, controllers). It is a pure deformation skeleton — great for an
